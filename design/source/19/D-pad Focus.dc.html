How the D-pad’s focus looks
It appears at the first key press, a touch hides it, and it moves to the next control in the direction pressed (ADR-0161). One element in a top layer draws it over the control’s box, so nothing around it moves or resizes, and no scrolling list can clip it. George chose the teal ring, 2b, and Jump (2026-10-25); the screens below are drawn in it.
Which ring
All three share one geometry: a 6 px band 4 px off the control, following its corners, with a 2 px dark keyline on each side. From 3 m one arc-minute is 0.87 mm. A pixel is 0.17 mm on the 10.1″ panel and 0.15 mm on the 7.9″ bar; on the 11.9″ bar, drawn at 0.8, a page pixel is 0.16 mm. The band is about 1 mm on all three, and 1.7 mm with its keylines.

Its rules
Outside
Round controls, buttons, tiles and covers. The ring sits 4 px out and its corners are the control’s radius plus 10.
Inset
Rows and anything else with less than 12 px to its neighbours. The ring is drawn 2 px inside the row, so it never covers the next one.
Sliders
The ring takes the whole track with an arrow at each end, because Left and Right change the value. The arrow pressed steps out 5 px and the other dims while the value moves.
Selection stays
A selected row keeps its teal, a tab its underline, a toggle its state. The focus is only ever the ring on top, its dark keylines keeping it apart from the teal under it.
On the Pi
The ring is one element with a border and two box-shadows of no blur, moved with transform. Nothing is read back from the screen.
How it travels
Right, every 1.2 s
Jump, chosen: the ring is drawn on the next control at once. Gliding, not taken, moves it there in 160 ms on the panel’s own curve, which helps the eye follow it across a wide gap; a held key skips the glide and jumps.
